Accessibility Statement

We treat accessibility as a design requirement rather than a remediation task. Last updated 01/05/2026.

Template notice

This statement is a starting template. Before publication, the conformance claim must be substantiated by an actual audit against WCAG 2.1 Level AA, including testing with screen readers and keyboard-only navigation, and reviewed by a real estate attorney licensed in each state where we operate.

Our commitment

Meridian Shore Realty, LLC is committed to making this website usable by as many people as possible, including people who use screen readers, magnification, speech input, switch access or keyboard-only navigation, and people with low vision, colour vision deficiency, motor impairments or cognitive disabilities.

This commitment is not separate from our fair housing obligations. Housing information that some people cannot reach is housing information that is not equally available, and we treat it that way.

Conformance target

We aim to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA. Where we have not yet met that standard, we say so under known limitations below.

What we have built in

  • Body text is never smaller than 18 pixels anywhere on this site, including footnotes, captions, form hints, button labels, navigation and legal copy. Small print is a barrier and we do not use it. The only text below that size is the 13 pixel uppercase category label used above some section headings and as table column headings, which is a short label rather than reading matter.
  • Text contrast meets or exceeds 4.5:1 against its background. Our brass accent colour measures below that against the page background, so it is used only as a fill, a rule or a border, never as a text colour. Where a brass-toned text colour is needed we use a darker variant that measures above 6:1.
  • Interactive elements are at least 48 pixels tall, including navigation links, buttons, form fields, gallery controls, card headings and standalone links. We verified this by measuring the rendered page at 360, 600, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440 and 1920 pixels rather than by assuming it. One deliberate exception: the checkbox control itself is 28 pixels, sitting inside a 48 pixel row whose label is clickable, so the actual target is the full row. 28 pixels already exceeds the 24 pixel minimum in WCAG 2.2 success criterion 2.5.8.
  • Links inside a sentence keep the normal text size and spacing. Enlarging them would break the line, and WCAG 2.2 explicitly exempts inline links from the target size requirement.
  • Focus is always visible. A three-pixel outline with an offset appears on every focusable element. We have not removed focus indicators anywhere.
  • The whole site works with a keyboard. The mobile menu traps focus while open, closes on Escape and returns focus to the button that opened it. The property gallery responds to the left and right arrow keys. Accordions are buttons with correct expanded state.
  • A skip-to-content link is the first focusable element on every page.
  • Semantic landmarks are used throughout: a single main region per page, header, navigation with labels, and footer. Headings are in order and describe the content beneath them.
  • Every image has descriptive alt text that describes what is actually in the photograph. Decorative thumbnails inside the gallery, which duplicate an adjacent labelled control, carry empty alt text so screen readers do not announce them twice.
  • No information is conveyed by colour alone. Saved properties, active navigation, form errors and validation states all carry text or shape cues as well.
  • No essential information is revealed only on hover. Everything reachable by hover is also reachable by keyboard and visible without it.
  • Forms have visible labels above every field, helper text where it is useful, and error messages placed below the field they relate to as well as summarised at the top. Errors are announced to assistive technology.
  • Motion is restrained and can be switched off. All animation is disabled under the operating system's reduced-motion preference. There is no parallax, no autoplay video, and no carousel that advances on its own.
  • Layout is stable. Every image carries width and height attributes so nothing shifts as the page loads.
  • No thin or light font weights are used anywhere on this site.
  • Text can be resized to 200 percent and the layout reflows without loss of content or function. Zoom is not disabled.
  • The site works at 360 pixels wide without horizontal scrolling of the page body. Wide tables scroll inside their own container rather than forcing the page sideways. Every page was measured in a real browser at 360, 599, 600, 768, 1023, 1024, 1239, 1280, 1440 and 1920 pixels to confirm the document never becomes wider than the viewport.
  • Layout breakpoints are derived from measurement. The horizontal navigation bar appears at 1280 pixels because that is the width at which it genuinely fits on one line; below that a menu button is used instead. Under 600 pixels the header becomes two rows so the telephone number stays visible without the header overflowing the screen.

Known limitations

We publish these rather than claiming a perfect result.

  • Third-party photographs. Images are served from a third-party host. If that host is unreachable, the alt text is what remains, which is one reason we write it properly.
  • Wide data tables. Climate and market tables scroll horizontally inside their container on narrow screens. This is preferable to shrinking the text, but it does require horizontal scrolling within that region.
  • Documents. Association documents, inspection reports and similar material originate with third parties and are frequently scanned images without a text layer. We cannot make a third party's scan accessible, but we will read any document to you, summarise it, or arrange it in another format on request.
  • Automated checks only so far. Layout, text size, target size and overflow have been measured programmatically in a real browser across ten viewport widths, and the defects that search found have been fixed. That is not the same as an accessibility audit. This site has not yet been reviewed by an independent specialist, and it has not been tested end to end with screen readers, voice control or switch access. Until that work is done, the conformance claim above is a target supported by measurement rather than a verified result.

Alternative formats and assistance

If anything on this site is not accessible to you, we will provide the information another way at no charge. That includes reading a page or a document to you by telephone, sending information in large print or plain text, arranging a meeting at an accessible location, or communicating through whatever method works for you.

You do not need to explain your disability or provide documentation to ask for this.

Reporting a barrier

Tell us and we will fix it.

Telephone: +1 (941) 555-0148
Post: Accessibility Contact, Meridian Shore Realty, LLC, 1420 Bayshore Boulevard, Suite 300, Sarasota, Florida 34236, United States

Please tell us the page address, what you were trying to do, and what happened. If you use assistive technology, telling us which one helps us reproduce the problem.

We aim to acknowledge within two business days and to give you the information you were trying to reach at the same time, rather than making you wait for a fix. Where a fix takes longer, we will tell you what we are doing and when we expect it to be done.

Visiting the office

Our office is on the third floor with lift access from the street-level lobby and step-free entry from the parking structure. Accessible parking is available on the ground level of the structure. There is an accessible restroom on the third floor. If you need any adjustment to meet with us comfortably, tell us when you arrange the meeting.

Ongoing work

Accessibility is checked as part of every change to this site rather than as a periodic audit. New pages are built to the same requirements as existing ones, and any barrier reported to us is treated as a defect rather than a request.

Real estate websites are a frequent subject of web accessibility litigation. That is not why we do this, but it is a reason no brokerage should treat the work as optional.
