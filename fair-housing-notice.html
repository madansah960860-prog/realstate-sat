Fair Housing Notice

Equal Housing Opportunity. What that means in practice, what the law protects, and how to complain if we or anyone else falls short.

Template notice

This notice is a starting template. It must be reviewed by a real estate attorney licensed in each state where we operate before it is relied on, and the protected classes listed must be confirmed against current federal, state and local law in every jurisdiction where we do business.

Our commitment

Meridian Shore Realty, LLC is committed to the letter and the spirit of United States policy for the achievement of equal housing opportunity throughout the nation. We comply with the federal Fair Housing Act, Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968 as amended, and with all applicable state and local fair housing laws.

We do not discriminate, and we do not permit any person acting on our behalf to discriminate, in the sale, rental, financing, advertising or brokerage of housing. Every person who contacts this brokerage receives the same standard of service, the same access to information, and the same willingness to represent them.

This obligation is not a formality on a footer. It governs how every page on this site is written and how every conversation in this brokerage is conducted.

Protected classes

Federal law

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing on the basis of:

  • Race
  • Color
  • Religion
  • Sex, which the Department of Housing and Urban Development interprets to include sexual orientation and gender identity
  • Familial status, meaning the presence of children under 18 in a household, pregnancy, and the process of securing legal custody of a child
  • National origin
  • Disability

State and local law

Many states and localities protect additional characteristics, which commonly include age, marital status, source of income including housing vouchers, military or veteran status, ancestry, sexual orientation and gender identity where not already covered, and lawful occupation.

The protected classes in Florida, Texas and Arizona, and in the individual counties and municipalities where we operate, must be confirmed against current law before this page goes live. Where a local ordinance protects more than state law does, we follow the ordinance.

How this changes what we write

Fair housing law restricts statements that indicate a preference, a limitation or discrimination. That applies to listing copy, community descriptions, advertising, social media and casual conversation.

So we describe the property and the amenities, and never the intended occupant.

If you ask us who lives in a community, we will decline and explain why. That is not evasiveness. Answering would breach the law and would also be a poor way to help you decide, because the question you actually want answered is usually about amenities, quiet hours, association rules or distance to something.

Demographic information about any area is publicly available from the United States Census Bureau, and you are free to consult it.

Age-restricted housing

Housing that lawfully restricts occupancy by age does so under a narrow exemption in the Housing for Older Persons Act, which requires that the community actually meet strict occupancy criteria and maintain verification procedures.

None of the communities described on this site is presented as age-restricted, and none of them makes an age-based occupancy claim. Where a community claims that status, we ask for its documentation, and we treat the claim as unverified until we have seen it. We do not apply the label casually and we do not use "55+", "active adult" or similar phrasing as marketing shorthand.

Advertising

Advertising campaigns for this website are self-identified as Housing ads with Google. That designation restricts targeting on the basis of age, gender, parental status, marital status and ZIP code, and limits audience targeting. Our advertising copy is written about properties, locations and services, never about who a buyer is, and it is reviewed against fair housing requirements before it runs.

Reasonable accommodation and reasonable modification

If you have a disability and need a reasonable accommodation in order to work with us, tell us and we will provide it. That includes materials in an alternative format, meeting at an accessible location, additional time, communication through a method that works for you, or the presence of a person assisting you.

Email accessibility@meridianshorerealty.com, call +1 (941) 555-0148, or tell any member of the team. You do not need to explain your disability or provide documentation to ask for a change to how we communicate with you.

Separately, fair housing law addresses reasonable accommodations in rules, policies and services, and reasonable modifications to a dwelling, in housing itself. If a community's rules present a barrier, we will help you understand the request process, and we will tell you where the question needs a lawyer.

See also our Accessibility Statement for this website.

How to file a complaint

With us

If you believe anyone at this brokerage has treated you unfairly, contact the managing broker directly. Email legal@meridianshorerealty.com or write to the office marked for the attention of the Managing Broker. We will investigate and respond in writing. Raising a concern with us does not affect your right to complain to HUD or to a state agency, and does not extend any filing deadline.

With the US Department of Housing and Urban Development

You may file a housing discrimination complaint with HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity. There is no charge to file, and you do not need a lawyer.

  • Online: hud.gov/fairhousing
  • By telephone: 1-800-669-9777, or TTY 1-800-877-8339
  • By post: Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity, U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, 451 7th Street SW, Washington, DC 20410

A complaint must generally be filed with HUD within one year of the alleged discriminatory act. A separate right to file a lawsuit in federal or state court generally exists within two years. Those deadlines are set by statute and can differ in particular circumstances, so speak to an attorney or a fair housing organisation promptly rather than waiting.

With a state agency

Each state has an agency that handles housing discrimination complaints, and many states have substantially equivalent enforcement arrangements with HUD. The correct agency, address and telephone number for Florida, Texas and Arizona must be confirmed and listed here before launch.

Free assistance

Fair housing organisations across the country provide free advice and assistance. HUD maintains a directory of fair housing organisations by state at hud.gov/fairhousing.
