DMCA and copyright notice

How to tell us about material on this site that infringes your copyright, and what happens next. Last updated 01/05/2026.

Template notice

This page is a starting template. It must be reviewed by a real estate attorney licensed in each state where we operate, together with intellectual property counsel, before it is relied on. A designated agent must be registered with the United States Copyright Office and the details below replaced with the registered agent's actual details.

Our position on copyright

We respect copyright and we expect the same of others. Real estate websites carry a particular risk here, because photographs move between agents, brokerages, portals and social media, and the person who took a photograph is frequently not the person who supplied it.

Photographs on this site are placeholder images pending replacement, or are used under licence. Photograph credits and licence terms must be confirmed for every image before launch. If you believe an image or any other material here infringes your copyright, tell us using the process below and we will act on it promptly.

If you are a photographer whose work has been used without permission, you do not need to send a formal legal notice to get our attention. An email describing the image and where it appears is enough for us to take it down while we investigate.

Sending a notice of claimed infringement

To be effective under section 512(c)(3) of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, a written notice must include substantially the following:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner, or a person authorised to act on the owner's behalf.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed. If multiple works at this site are covered by one notice, a representative list of those works.
  3. Identification of the material that is claimed to be infringing and that is to be removed, with information reasonably sufficient to let us locate it. A full page address and a description of the specific image or passage is the most useful form of this.
  4. Information reasonably sufficient to let us contact you: name, postal address, telephone number and email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good faith belief that use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or are authorised to act on the owner's behalf.

Send the notice to our designated agent, below. An incomplete notice may delay our response, and we may come back to you for the missing element rather than ignoring it.

What we do when we receive a notice

  1. We acknowledge receipt, normally within two business days.
  2. We remove or disable access to the material identified, promptly, without waiting to resolve the merits.
  3. We take reasonable steps to notify whoever supplied the material that it has been removed and why.
  4. We keep a record of the notice and of what we did.

Removing material is not an admission that a claim is correct. It is what the statute contemplates while the question is worked out.

Counter-notification

If your material was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, you may send a counter-notification containing substantially the following:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed and the location at which it appeared before removal.
  3. A statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, postal address and telephone number.
  5. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district in which your address is located, or, if your address is outside the United States, of any judicial district in which we may be found, and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice or that person's agent.

If we receive a valid counter-notification, we will forward it to the original complainant and may restore the material after the statutory waiting period unless the complainant notifies us that they have filed an action seeking a court order.

Misrepresentation

Section 512(f) of the Act provides that a person who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages including costs and legal fees. Do not send a notice to remove material you simply dislike. Copyright is not a general-purpose takedown mechanism, and a notice about content that is accurate but unflattering is not a copyright claim.

Repeat infringers

We terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts or access of anyone who repeatedly infringes copyright, including contributors, vendors and photographers who supply us with material they do not have the right to license.

Trade marks

The Meridian Shore Realty name and wordmark are our trade marks. Third-party names and marks referred to on this site belong to their owners and are used only to identify them. If you believe your trade mark has been misused on this site, contact legal@meridianshorerealty.com with the mark, the registration details if any, and where it appears.

Listing content and MLS data

Once live listing data is connected, it will be supplied under a data licence from the relevant multiple listing service and will remain subject to that service's rules on display, attribution and removal. If you are a listing broker or a seller and believe content relating to your listing is displayed here in breach of those rules, contact us and we will remove it while we check.

If you are unsure

Email legal@meridianshorerealty.com and describe the problem in plain language. We would rather resolve a copyright question informally and quickly than receive a formal notice a month later.
