Four communities, researched the same way

We cover a small number of places properly rather than a long list superficially. Each page below carries the same structure so you can compare them side by side without re-reading marketing copy.

What is on every community page

Location and setting. Property types and the sizes actually built there. Current asking-price range. Association or community fee range and what the fee covers. Amenities and their operating seasons. Climate month by month. Distance to the nearest international airport and to the nearest hospital with a full emergency department. How you get around without a car, stated as real distances rather than vague phrases. And a section on what a normal week there actually involves, including the parts people find inconvenient.

We describe the property and the amenities. We do not describe who a community is for. That is both a legal requirement under the Fair Housing Act and, frankly, a better way to write: you are the one deciding whether a place suits you.

How to use these pages

Read the Seasonal Living Facts strip first. Five numbers on that strip decide more purchases than any photograph: winter temperature, airport distance, county property tax rate, fee range and the months the community is actually occupied.

If those five numbers work for you, read the rest. If they do not, move on quickly.

Read this before comparing tax rates

Effective property tax rates are not comparable across states without also comparing what the state charges elsewhere, how assessments are set, how often they are reset, and which exemptions you would actually qualify for. Texas rates look high next to Arizona rates for reasons that have nothing to do with the house. This is a question for a licensed CPA who can look at your whole picture, not a reason to prefer one state.

Not seeing the place you have in mind?
