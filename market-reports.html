Market reports

Quarterly reports on the second-home markets we cover. They are free, they carry no obligation, and they exist because they generate consultation enquiries. We would rather say that than pretend otherwise.

What these reports do

They report what has already happened: closed sales, median and average closed price, price per square foot, active inventory, months of supply, days on market and the ratio of closed price to last list price. Where the data supports it we split single-family from attached, and waterfront from non-waterfront, because blending them produces a number that describes nothing.

What they deliberately do not do

They contain no forecast. We do not predict prices, we do not tell you it is a good time to buy or sell, and we do not describe any market as an opportunity. Nobody can forecast a housing market reliably, and a brokerage that publishes predictions is marketing rather than reporting.

They also contain no unverifiable superlatives. You will not find this brokerage described as the leading anything, and you will not find a community described as the best anywhere. Where we hold an opinion we label it as an opinion.

Methodology

  • Source: the relevant multiple listing service for each market, as of the stated data date.
  • Closed sales only. Pending and under-contract figures are stated separately where used.
  • Property types separated rather than blended.
  • Medians reported alongside averages, because a small number of large sales distorts an average in thin markets.
  • Sample sizes stated. Where a quarter's sample is small we say so rather than reporting a percentage change from it.
  • Revisions happen. Late-reported sales change prior quarters and we restate rather than quietly adjusting.

All figures in the current reports are illustrative placeholders pending a licensed MLS data agreement. MLS rules govern attribution and permitted use of this data.

Reports for other markets

We publish for the markets where we are licensed and actively transacting. For the upper Texas coast we currently report inside client consultations rather than publicly, because the sample sizes in the specific waterfront submarkets are too small for a public quarterly report to be meaningful. If you want the Galveston County numbers, ask and we will send what we have with the sample sizes attached.

Ask for a market you do not see

How to read a market report without being misled

Check the sample size before the percentage. A 22 percent rise in median price on nine sales tells you almost nothing. In thin luxury submarkets a single large transaction moves the median.

Median and average tell different stories. When the average rises and the median does not, the mix has changed: more large sales, not broadly higher prices.

Months of supply is more informative than price. It describes the balance between buyers and sellers directly, and it moves before price does.

Days on market is frequently understated. Relisting resets the clock in many systems. Look at cumulative days where the data supports it.

Seasonality is not a trend. In these markets, comparing the fourth quarter to the third quarter measures the calendar rather than the market. Compare a quarter with the same quarter a year earlier.

Why we publish them

Directly, because people who read a market report and find it useful sometimes book a consultation, and consultations are how this brokerage gets clients. That is the business reason and there is no need to dress it up.

Indirectly, because writing them keeps us honest. Assembling the numbers each quarter is what stops a brokerage drifting into telling clients what the market feels like rather than what it did.

There is no charge, no email required to read a report on this site, and no download gate. If you want the reports by email when they are published you can ask for that, and every email will carry a working unsubscribe link and our physical mailing address.
