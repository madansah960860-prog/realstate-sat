Fee-based service

Relocation and downsizing concierge

A flat-fee service that coordinates the move itself: contractors, utilities, storage, estate sales, deliveries and the first-season punch list. It is separate from brokerage representation, it is optional, and the prices are published below rather than quoted case by case.

Why this exists

Buying a house in a state you do not live in creates a coordination problem that a brokerage relationship does not solve. Somebody has to be present when the air conditioning contractor arrives, know which utility serves which side of the street, be at the house for the furniture delivery, and notice that the water heater is leaking in week three.

Some clients have family nearby, or the time to fly back repeatedly, and do not need this. Some do not. We built the service for the second group and we price it as a service rather than folding it into a commission, because bundling it would mean the clients who do not use it pay for the clients who do.

How it is billed

Flat fees, agreed in writing before any work starts, invoiced half at engagement and half at completion. The fee is separate from any brokerage commission and is not contingent on a transaction. You can engage the concierge service without buying through us, and you can buy through us without engaging the concierge service.

Third-party costs, meaning the movers, the storage, the contractors, the utility deposits, are paid by you directly to those vendors. We do not mark them up and we do not take a percentage of them. Where a vendor pays us a referral fee, we tell you at the point we recommend them.

Referral relationship disclosure

Some moving companies, storage operators and estate-sale companies on our partner list pay Meridian Shore Realty a referral fee. Where one does, we disclose it in the same conversation as the recommendation and we will tell you the amount.

We do not accept referral fees from lenders, home inspectors, insurance agents, attorneys or accountants, because in those roles the independence of the recommendation matters more than the revenue.

How an engagement runs

  1. Scoping call

    Forty-five minutes, no charge

    We establish what you actually need. Roughly a third of the people who ask about this service leave the call having decided they do not need it, and we tell them so. There is no obligation and no follow-up sequence.

  2. Written scope

    A fixed scope and a fixed fee

    Before anything begins you receive a written scope listing every task, the fee, what is excluded, the duration, and the named person responsible. Nothing starts until you sign it.

  3. Vendor selection

    Three quotes, and every relationship disclosed

    For each significant vendor category we obtain at least three quotes and present them side by side. Where a vendor pays us a referral fee, that is stated on the comparison sheet next to their name, not in a footnote.

  4. Execution

    One named coordinator, weekly written updates

    You deal with one person throughout, not a queue. You receive a short written update every week with what has been done, what is scheduled and what needs a decision from you.

  5. Handover

    A document set you keep

    At completion you receive a folder containing every warranty, manual, permit, contractor contact, utility account number, association credential and photograph taken during the process. Clients tell us this is the part they use most in year two.

Moving between states, practically

Interstate household moves are federally regulated. A mover must give you written estimates, a booklet on your rights, and a bill of lading. Estimates come as binding, non-binding or binding not-to-exceed, and the difference matters: a non-binding estimate can rise on the day when the load is weighed.

Valuation is not insurance. The default liability level on an interstate move is calculated by weight and is very low relative to the value of household goods. Full value protection costs more and is usually worth it. Read what is excluded, because high-value items generally have to be listed individually to be covered at all.

Booking windows matter. Late spring through summer is the busy season nationally, and in our markets the October and November window fills early because that is when seasonal households arrive. Book eight to twelve weeks ahead where you can.

Finally, check the mover's registration and complaint history through the federal database before you sign anything. It takes five minutes and it is the single most useful piece of diligence in the whole process.

Seasonal close-up and open-up

The add-on most existing clients keep year to year. Twice a year we work the property's written close-up or open-up list, photograph the result, and send you the report. Water off, systems set, shutters checked, storm plan confirmed with the home-watch company on the way out; systems back on, air conditioning verified, water tested and the house aired on the way in.

It is not a substitute for a home-watch contract during the months you are away, and we will say so rather than sell you something that leaves a gap.
