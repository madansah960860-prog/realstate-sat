Azure Key Residences

Three eleven-storey buildings on a barrier island, completed between 2004 and 2011. Residences of 1,100 to 2,650 square feet with a deeded beach easement, currently asking $520,000 to $2,150,000.

Location and setting

Azure Key occupies a 6.2-acre parcel on the Gulf side of a barrier island in Pinellas County. The three buildings are set back roughly 210 feet from the mean high water line behind a vegetated dune that the association is required to maintain under a state permit. The intracoastal waterway is 0.3 miles east, and the mainland is reached by a two-lane bascule bridge that lifts on demand for tall vessels.

The parcel sits between 6 and 9 feet above sea level and is inside the coastal high-hazard area. Every unit is in a designated flood zone. Buildings are constructed with the ground level given over to covered parking and storage rather than living space, which is the standard coastal design response and the reason the elevated floors carry very different insurance exposure from the ground level.

The island is fully built out. There is no vacant developable land within a mile in either direction along the beach, which means the view corridors from the upper floors are unlikely to change. It also means every construction project you will see for the next decade is a rebuild rather than a new site.

Property types available

Six floor plans across three buildings. All units have covered parking, private balconies and impact-rated glazing installed as original construction rather than retrofitted.

  • Plan A, 1,100 square feet, two bedrooms and two bathrooms, north and south exposures, no direct Gulf view from the living area.
  • Plan B, 1,480 square feet, two bedrooms plus a den, direct Gulf exposure on floors 3 to 9.
  • Plan C, 1,760 square feet, three bedrooms, corner position with two balconies.
  • Plan D, 2,180 square feet, three bedrooms and three bathrooms, wrap balcony, floors 4 to 10 only.
  • Plan E, 2,420 square feet, three bedrooms with a separate service entrance and two parking spaces.
  • Penthouse plan, 2,650 square feet, eleventh floor only, six units in total, private roof terrace.

All plans are single-level with zero-step entry from the elevator lobby, and all doorways on the primary circulation path are 36 inches. Two of the three buildings have three elevators; the third has two, which is noticeable in season.

Fees, reserves and inspections

The condominium fee runs $1,050 to $2,380 per month depending on plan and building. It covers the master insurance programme including wind, building maintenance and reserves, water and sewer, cable and internet, the pools, the fitness room, the dune maintenance plan, elevator service and staffed reception in season.

Two Florida requirements matter more here than anywhere else on this site. Buildings of three storeys or more must complete a milestone structural inspection at defined intervals, and associations must fund reserves for structural components based on a structural integrity reserve study rather than waiving them by member vote. Both requirements have raised budgets across coastal Florida since 2022, in some buildings substantially.

Before making an offer, ask for the milestone inspection report, the structural integrity reserve study, the current reserve balances by component, and any board resolution about a special assessment. A building that has completed its inspection and funded its reserves is in a very different position from one that has not, and the difference shows up in the price you should be willing to pay.

Insurance, stated plainly

Coastal condominium insurance in Florida is expensive and has been volatile. The association carries the master policy on the building structure and common elements; you carry a separate unit-owner policy covering interior finishes, contents, liability and loss assessment. Flood is a separate policy again, and the association's flood coverage does not extend to the inside of your unit.

Get a written insurance quote for the specific unit before your inspection period ends. Quotes vary widely by floor, by building age and by whether the glazing is impact-rated. Do not rely on the seller's premium as a guide to yours.

Amenities and their operating seasons

A heated pool and spa open year-round, a second unheated pool that closes from December to February, a fitness room open at all hours by fob, two social rooms available for private booking, a car wash bay, kayak racks with a waiting list, and a boardwalk to the beach easement with an outdoor rinse station. Reception is staffed daily from November to April and on weekdays only from May to October. There is no restaurant on site.

Condominium documents to request

  • Declaration of condominium and all amendments
  • Milestone structural inspection report and remediation status
  • Structural integrity reserve study and current reserve balances
  • Current budget and two years of audited financials
  • Master insurance declarations page, wind and flood
  • Board minutes for the last five years
  • Rental and occupancy rules and the approval process
  • Estoppel certificate at contract
  • Pet, vehicle and balcony-use rules

Florida gives condominium buyers a statutory document review and cancellation period. Use all of it.

Airports and hospitals

Tampa International (TPA) is 24 miles east, typically thirty-five to fifty-five minutes depending on the bridge and on season. It is a large hub with year-round nonstop service across the country and a handful of transatlantic routes. St Pete-Clearwater International (PIE) is 12 miles northeast and is served primarily by one low-cost carrier with a route map that changes seasonally.

The nearest hospital with a twenty-four hour emergency department is 5.1 miles from the property on the mainland, about fourteen minutes when the bridge is down. That qualifier matters: the bascule bridge lifts on demand, and emergency services plan around it, but you should understand that your route to the mainland crosses a drawbridge. A larger hospital with cardiac and stroke certification is 9.8 miles east.

Getting around

The island has a continuous sidewalk along the main road and a marked bicycle lane. Distances from the lobby: nearest grocery 1.4 miles, pharmacy 1.2 miles, hardware store 3.9 miles, nearest restaurant cluster 0.5 miles, public beach access with parking 0.2 miles, and the nearest post office 2.1 miles. A good number of residents use a bicycle or a golf cart for most island errands and keep a car for the mainland.

The Pinellas Suncoast Transit Authority runs a route along the island road with roughly hourly weekday service and a seasonal beach trolley that runs more frequently between November and April. Ride-hailing is reliable year-round here because the island is close to a dense mainland population.

Parking is the standing island frustration. Each residence has assigned covered parking, but guest parking is limited and the public beach lots fill by mid-morning on winter weekends.

What daily life here actually involves

Between November and April the building is largely occupied, the pool deck has a rhythm to it, and the social rooms are booked most weeks. Traffic on the island road slows considerably on weekends and the bridge queue can add fifteen minutes at the wrong hour. Restaurants within a mile take reservations and use them.

Between June and September occupancy drops sharply. Afternoon thunderstorms are near-daily. Beach conditions are generally excellent in the morning and unpleasant by mid-afternoon. Red tide, a naturally occurring algal bloom, appears in some years along this coast and can make the beach unusable for days or weeks at a time. It is unpredictable, it is not caused by the community, and no one can promise you a year without it. Check the state's monitoring data for the specific stretch of coast before you commit.

Hurricane season runs from June to November. This community sits in a mandatory evacuation zone. That is a fact about the geography, not a defect in the building, and it means you need a plan: someone to close the unit, storm shutters or impact glass on any opening that lacks it, a place to move a vehicle, and a realistic expectation that you may be unable to return for several days after a storm makes landfall nearby.

Owners who are away for months use a home-watch service, run the air conditioning on a humidity setpoint rather than switching it off, and shut off the water supply at the unit valve. The association requires a local emergency contact on file for every unit.

Rental rules

The declaration permits a minimum lease term of ninety days with a maximum of two leases per year, and all tenants must be approved by the association. Short-term vacation rental is prohibited. If rental income is part of your plan, this community will not support a weekly rental model, and you should look elsewhere on the island where the zoning and documents allow it.
