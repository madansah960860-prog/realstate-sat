Aldermere Golf Club

A 640-acre community eleven miles inland from the Gulf, built between 1997 and 2016 around two eighteen-hole courses. Single-family homes, coach homes and villas, currently asking $685,000 to $3,400,000.

Location and setting

Aldermere sits east of the coastal ridge in Collier County, on flat former agricultural land that was platted in the mid-1990s. The nearest Gulf beach access is 11.4 miles west, roughly a twenty-two minute drive outside season and closer to thirty-five minutes in February when the seasonal traffic pattern is at its heaviest. The community is bounded on two sides by a preserve tract that cannot be developed, which is the main reason the western holes stay quiet.

The site is entirely flat, sitting between 9 and 14 feet above sea level. That elevation puts most of the community outside the coastal high-hazard area but does not put it outside the flood map entirely, and lenders will require a flood determination. Several streets on the southern edge sit in a designated flood zone with an insurance requirement attached. We check the exact zone by parcel, not by community, because it changes street to street.

Development finished in 2016. There is no builder inventory, no ongoing construction traffic and no unsold-lot risk, which also means there is no new-construction option here. Everything trading is resale, and roughly a third of the housing stock has had a substantial renovation since 2018.

Property types available

Four housing types were built, and they behave quite differently on resale.

  • Single-family estate homes on 0.25 to 0.6 acre lots, 2,900 to 5,400 square feet, mostly built 2003 to 2016. Private pools are standard rather than optional.
  • Single-family village homes on smaller lots, 1,850 to 2,900 square feet, built 1997 to 2007. Some have pools, some share a village pool.
  • Coach homes, two-storey buildings of four units each with attached garages, 1,600 to 2,400 square feet. The ground-floor units have single-level floor plans with zero-step entry from the garage.
  • Attached villas, paired one-storey units of 1,450 to 1,900 square feet, the lowest-fee and lowest-maintenance option in the community.

Coach homes and villas carry higher monthly fees because the association maintains the building envelope, roof and exterior paint. Estate homes carry lower fees but you own the roof. Over a ten-year hold the difference is smaller than it looks on a monthly statement, and roof replacement costs in Southwest Florida have moved sharply since 2022.

Fees, and what they actually cover

There are two separate financial obligations here, and confusing them is the most common mistake buyers make in golf communities.

The master association fee runs $780 to $1,450 per month depending on housing type. It covers gate staffing, road maintenance inside the gates, common landscaping, the preserve management plan, the community pools and the fitness building, and reserve contributions. For coach homes and villas it also covers roof and exterior maintenance and the building insurance policy.

The club membership is a separate contract with the club, not with the association, and it is not automatic on purchase. Golf membership requires an initiation deposit and annual dues, and there is a waiting list in most years. A social membership without golf access is available at a lower cost. Ask for the current membership plan document and the transfer terms before you make an offer, because the club's rules on transfer at resale materially affect what your home is worth to the next buyer.

Special assessments are separate from both. Ask for the last five years of association minutes and the most recent reserve study. Florida law now requires structural integrity reserve studies for many buildings, and the results have changed budgets across the state.

Amenities and their operating seasons

Two eighteen-hole courses, both open year-round, with the north course closing for eight to ten days of aerification in late summer. A practice range and short-game area. Ten tennis courts, six of them clay and lit until 10pm. Eight pickleball courts added in 2021. A resort pool with a café that runs limited hours from May to October, and a separate 25-yard lap pool that stays open year-round. A fitness building with a staffed desk from October through April and card access outside those months. A dog park on the eastern edge. A clubhouse dining room that closes for four weeks in late summer.

The reduced summer schedule is normal for this part of Florida and worth planning around if you intend to be here outside the winter months.

Climate by season

Southwest Florida has a dry season and a wet season rather than four distinct seasons. The practical difference for a homeowner is humidity, rainfall and storm exposure.

Documents to request before an offer

  • Declaration of covenants and all recorded amendments
  • Current year budget and the last two audited financial statements
  • Most recent reserve study and structural integrity reserve study
  • Five years of board and membership meeting minutes
  • Insurance certificate for the master policy, with wind and flood detail
  • Club membership plan, dues schedule and transfer terms
  • Rental and occupancy rules, including minimum lease terms
  • Any pending litigation disclosure

Airports and hospitals

Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW) is 38 miles north, typically a forty-five to sixty-five minute drive depending on season. It carries year-round nonstop service to most large Northeast and Midwest markets, with additional seasonal routes between December and April that disappear in summer. Naples Municipal (APF) is 13 miles west and handles general aviation only.

Tampa International (TPA) is 155 miles north and is worth the drive when a fare difference is large, but not otherwise. Miami International (MIA) is 118 miles east across Alligator Alley, which is a straightforward but monotonous two-hour drive.

The nearest hospital with a twenty-four hour emergency department is 8.6 miles west, about seventeen minutes outside season. A second hospital with a certified stroke and cardiac programme is 14 miles northwest. Two freestanding emergency departments and a network of urgent-care clinics sit within six miles. Verify current service lines directly with the hospital, as programme certifications change.

Getting around

This is a car-dependent community and there is no honest way to describe it otherwise. Distances from the main gate: nearest full grocery 2.3 miles, pharmacy 2.1 miles, hardware store 3.4 miles, the nearest restaurant cluster 2.6 miles, and the nearest public library branch 4.1 miles. There is a sidewalk network inside the gates and a paved multi-use path along the boundary road, but the connecting arterial has no continuous pedestrian route.

Golf carts are permitted on interior roads and on the boundary road as far as the shopping plaza, which is how a good number of residents make the grocery run. Collier Area Transit operates a fixed-route bus on the arterial with roughly hourly service on weekdays; the nearest stop is 0.7 miles from the gate. Ride-hailing coverage is reliable in season and thinner in August.

What daily life here actually involves

From November to April the community is close to fully occupied and the amenity schedule is dense: organised golf leagues most mornings, tennis and pickleball ladders, a card room that is genuinely busy, and a dining room that requires a reservation on weekends. Traffic on the arterial roughly doubles. Restaurant waits in Naples on a Friday in February can run past ninety minutes without a booking.

From June to September the picture inverts. Perhaps a quarter of homes are occupied. The dining room closes for part of the summer, the café runs short hours, and afternoon thunderstorms arrive with enough regularity that most outdoor activity happens before noon. Landscape crews run constantly because everything grows fast in the wet season.

Owners who are away for part of the year almost all use a home-watch service. Expect to pay for weekly interior checks, running the water, checking the air conditioning condensate line, and a post-storm inspection. Leaving a Florida house sealed and unattended in August without a functioning dehumidification strategy is how mould claims start.

Storm preparation is a real annual task. Most homes here have impact glass or accordion shutters; some have neither and rely on panels that take two people several hours to install. Ask which system the house has and when it was last operated, because a shutter system that has not been cycled in three years frequently does not close.

The community has a quiet-hours rule, a vehicle and boat storage rule that prohibits trailers on driveways, and an architectural review requirement for exterior changes including paint colour and roof material. These are ordinary for a community of this type but they are enforced, and they are worth reading before you plan a renovation.

Rental rules

Minimum lease term is thirty days with a maximum of three separate leases per calendar year, and tenants must be registered with the association. Club amenities do not automatically transfer to a tenant. If seasonal rental income is part of your plan, read the rental provisions in the declaration in full before you commit, and confirm the county tourist development tax registration requirement.
