What the numbers describe

The clearest pattern in this data set is the divergence between detached and attached property. Single-family closed volume rose while the median closed price fell slightly, and inventory rose by roughly thirty percent. Attached property moved in the opposite direction on volume, with a larger fall in median price, a much larger rise in inventory and days on market up sharply.

Median days on market rose in every segment and in both property types. The closed-to-last-list ratio fell in both. Taken together those two measures describe a market in which sellers are meeting buyers rather than the reverse, across the board.

The price bands show the pattern most people expect: liquidity thins as price rises. At the top of the market twenty-five closed sales in a quarter is a small enough sample that a percentage change from it is not a reliable signal about anything, which is why we report the count next to it.

We are describing what happened. We are not saying what happens next. A market can look exactly like this and go in either direction.

Two structural factors specific to this market

Insurance. Property insurance cost in coastal Collier and Lee counties has been a material factor in transaction decisions for several years. It affects attached property in a particular way, because the building's master policy sits inside the association fee, and a large premium increase arrives as a fee increase for every owner simultaneously rather than as an individual renewal. Several of the transactions our own clients did not complete this quarter ended on the insurance number rather than on the price.

Condominium reserve and inspection requirements. Florida's milestone structural inspection and structural integrity reserve study requirements have changed the financial position of a great many buildings in this region. Buildings that have completed inspections and funded reserves are in a materially different position from those that have not, and the difference shows in days on market at the individual building level in a way that is invisible in a county-level median.

This is the main reason we tell clients that a county median is close to useless for deciding what to pay for a specific unit in a specific building. The building's documents matter more than the county's numbers.

What this report cannot tell you

  • What any specific property is worth. That requires an appraisal or a comparative analysis of that property.
  • Where prices go next. We do not forecast and we do not repeat other people's forecasts.
  • Whether now is a good time for you to buy or sell. That depends on your circumstances, not on the market.
  • What is happening inside a single community. County-level data hides community-level differences that are frequently larger than the county-level changes.
