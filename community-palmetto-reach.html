Palmetto Reach

Canal and open-bay homesites with private docks on protected water off Galveston Bay. Most homes are raised on piers above base flood elevation. Currently asking $465,000 to $2,900,000.

Location and setting

Palmetto Reach sits on a peninsula of made ground on the western side of Galveston Bay, cut with four navigable canals that connect to open bay through a single channel. The community was platted in stages between 1984 and 2003, and a substantial share of the housing stock has been rebuilt since 2009, largely because of storm damage.

Ground elevation runs between 5 and 11 feet. Every lot is in a mapped flood zone, most in a zone that carries a mandatory flood insurance requirement for a federally backed mortgage. Newer homes are built on piers with the living level well above base flood elevation and the ground level given over to parking and storage. Older slab-on-grade homes remain, and they are a materially different insurance and resale proposition. Get the elevation certificate for the specific house. Without it you cannot price the insurance, and without the insurance number you cannot price the house.

The bay here is shallow, brackish and productive. Water clarity is not Caribbean and never has been; anyone selling you that picture is selling you something. What it is, is protected water with easy access to open bay, good fishing, and a serious recreational boating culture.

Property types available

  • Canal homes, 1,900 to 3,600 square feet on 60 to 90 foot waterfrontages, most with a private dock and boat lift. The canals carry roughly 5 to 7 feet of water at mean tide, which limits draft.
  • Open-bay homes, 2,600 to 5,800 square feet on larger lots facing the bay directly, with longer piers and bulkheads. These carry the highest wind and surge exposure and the highest insurance cost.
  • Interior homes, 1,500 to 2,800 square feet on non-waterfront lots one or two streets back, with access to the community ramp and dry storage rather than a private dock.
  • Vacant waterfront lots, a small number remaining, sold with bulkhead condition disclosed separately from the land.

Bulkhead condition is the single most expensive hidden variable in this community. A failing bulkhead on an open-bay lot can be a six-figure repair, and it is the owner's responsibility, not the association's. Have it inspected specifically. A general home inspection will not assess it.

Fees and dock rules

The association fee runs $210 to $540 per month depending on lot type. It covers channel marker maintenance, the community boat ramp and dry storage yard, common landscape, street lighting, the entry structure and reserves. It does not cover your dock, your lift, your bulkhead or your flood insurance.

Docks are regulated by the association's dock standard and separately by state and federal permitting for any structure over navigable water. Extensions, lifts and covered slips require approval and, in some cases, a permit from the US Army Corps of Engineers and the Texas General Land Office. If a listing advertises a dock, ask to see the permit. Unpermitted structures over water are a common and expensive problem in Gulf Coast communities.

Insurance, stated plainly

You will typically carry three separate policies here: a homeowner policy that in many coastal Texas cases excludes windstorm, a separate windstorm and hail policy which along this coast is frequently written through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association, and a flood policy. Some carriers bundle differently; the point is that wind and flood are usually not in your base homeowner policy.

Windstorm coverage generally requires certification that the structure meets the applicable building standard. For a rebuilt or renovated home, the certificate matters. Ask for it in writing during the option period and get a bindable quote for the specific address before that period expires.

Amenities

A two-lane community boat ramp with a floating dock and a wash-down station. A fenced dry storage yard for trailers and small craft, with a waiting list in spring. A fishing pier at the channel entrance. A pavilion with covered tables. A kayak launch. There is no pool, no golf and no clubhouse dining. Restaurants and marinas are clustered 2.8 miles north along the waterfront.

Climate by season

The upper Texas coast is humid subtropical, with a mild winter, a long humid summer and a genuine hurricane exposure. Winter here is cooler than Florida or Arizona: hard freezes are infrequent but they happen, and the February 2021 event caused widespread pipe failures across the region. Freeze protection on exposed plumbing is a real requirement for a house that sits empty.

Documents and reports to request

  • Recorded restrictions, dock standard and all amendments
  • Current budget, reserve study and reserve balances
  • Elevation certificate for the specific structure
  • Flood insurance quote and prior claims history for the address
  • Windstorm certification and a bindable windstorm quote
  • Bulkhead inspection by a marine contractor, not a home inspector
  • Dock, lift and slip permits
  • Survey showing the bulkhead line and any encroachment
  • Seller disclosure covering prior flooding and repairs

Airports and hospitals

William P. Hobby Airport (HOU) is 44 miles northwest, generally fifty to seventy-five minutes and considerably longer in Houston peak traffic. George Bush Intercontinental (IAH) is 62 miles north and carries a far wider international route map, at the cost of a longer and less predictable drive. Scholes International at Galveston (GLS) is 26 miles south and handles general aviation.

The nearest hospital with a twenty-four hour emergency department is 6.4 miles northwest, roughly thirteen minutes. A larger medical centre with cardiac and trauma capability is 18 miles north. The Texas Medical Center in Houston, one of the largest medical complexes in the country, is 42 miles north and is a genuine practical advantage of this location for anyone with a complex ongoing medical relationship.

Getting around

Car-dependent. Distances from the community entrance: nearest full grocery 3.2 miles, pharmacy 3.0 miles, hardware store 2.6 miles, nearest restaurant and marina cluster 2.8 miles, nearest public library 5.4 miles. There is a sidewalk on the entry road for the first 0.6 miles and then none. There is no fixed-route transit serving the community; the nearest park-and-ride into Houston is 12 miles northwest.

Boat access is the alternative transport here in a real sense: a number of residents run to the restaurant and marina cluster by water rather than by road, and there is guest dockage there.

What daily life here actually involves

From October to May the community is close to fully occupied and the weather is genuinely pleasant for most of it. Fishing is the organising activity: the bay runs a seasonal pattern, and the community pier and ramp are busy at dawn. Winter cold fronts arrive every week or so, drop the temperature twenty degrees in an afternoon and blow hard out of the north for a day, which flattens the bay and stops boating briefly.

From June to September the heat and humidity are constant, the wind drops, and mosquito pressure near the marsh edge is significant enough that most homes have screened outdoor space. Boating shifts to early morning and evening.

Hurricane exposure is the defining fact of this location and we will not soften it. This community is in a coastal evacuation zone. Storm surge, not wind, is the primary risk on a shallow bay, and surge does not respect a well-built house. Owners here plan for it: raised living levels, impact glazing or shutters, a written storm plan, a haul-out arrangement for the boat, and insurance that is actually in force with the correct wind and flood components. Rebuilding after a major storm on this coast has historically taken a long time because contractor capacity is consumed regionally all at once.

For an owner who is away in summer, the standing tasks are: someone to close and shutter the house on a named-storm watch, a boat plan, air conditioning maintained on a humidity setpoint, water shut off at the main, and freeze protection checked before the first winter front. Home-watch services here are common and inexpensive relative to the risk.

Rental rules

The recorded restrictions permit leases of thirty days or longer and prohibit rentals of under thirty days. Tenants must be registered and the owner remains responsible for compliance. Some neighbouring communities on this coast permit short-term rental and some municipalities regulate it separately, so do not assume the rules on one street apply to the next.
