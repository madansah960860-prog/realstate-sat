Client stories

Four accounts of how representation has worked in practice, including two where our advice was not to proceed.

Every story on this page is a placeholder

The accounts below are illustrative and were written to show the shape of the work. They are not real clients and the quotes are not real testimonials. Before launch, every story here must be replaced with a verified account from an actual client who has given written consent to its publication, or removed. Publishing invented testimonials is deceptive, it breaches advertising law, and it is not something we would do.

Past outcomes described here, real or illustrative, do not indicate what will happen in any other transaction.

Purchase · Collier County, Florida

A golf community, after two years of looking

The clients had been researching Southwest Florida for two years before the first consultation and had already ruled out four communities on their own. What they had not done was compare carrying cost, and the shortlist they arrived with contained two properties whose annual running costs differed by roughly twenty-six thousand dollars at the same asking price.

The difference was mostly roof age, glazing and the club membership structure. One community's club transfer terms meant a buyer had to take a full golf membership with an initiation deposit whether or not they played; the other allowed a social membership. That single provision was worth more than every finish difference between the two houses.

They bought the second one. The transaction took four months from consultation to closing, with a twenty-one day inspection period because the association document package ran to six hundred pages.

Advised against · Pinellas County, Florida

The condominium we told them not to buy

A Gulf-front residence at a price that looked, on the face of it, like good value against the building next door. The finishes were excellent and the view was the one the clients wanted.

The building's milestone structural inspection had identified remediation to the parking structure and the balcony assemblies. The association had not adopted a funding plan, reserves stood at a fraction of what the structural integrity reserve study indicated, and the minutes from the previous eighteen months showed the board repeatedly deferring a decision. Two lenders declined the project on review.

We advised against proceeding, and explained that the same facts which made it difficult to finance would narrow the buyer pool when they eventually came to sell. They withdrew during the document review period and bought in a different building nine months later.

Purchase and sale · Arizona and out of state

A lot, a build, and a sale in a state where we are not licensed

The clients wanted to build rather than buy, on a hillside lot in the northeast valley, and needed to sell a house in a state where we hold no licence.

On the lot, the work was slope analysis, the natural area open space calculation and the disturbance envelope. Two lots they had shortlisted at similar prices had usable areas that differed by nearly forty percent, which is not visible from a plat map or a photograph.

On the sale, we referred them to a brokerage licensed in that state and disclosed our referral fee, the percentage and who paid it, in writing before they spoke to the receiving agent. That fee came from the receiving brokerage's commission and did not increase what the clients paid.

Advised against · Galveston County, Texas

The waterfront house that failed on insurance

An open-bay property with a private dock, a recent renovation and an asking price that fitted the clients' budget comfortably.

Three things came back during the option period. The windstorm certification for the renovation could not be produced. The bulkhead, inspected by a marine contractor rather than a home inspector, had section failure that the general inspection had not identified. And the bindable windstorm and flood quotes together came to roughly double what the clients had budgeted.

Any one of those was negotiable. All three together, on a house that had already been renovated once, was a different proposition. They terminated within the option period. Six months later they bought a canal property in the same community with a lower exposure profile and an insurance figure they could live with.

Why two of the four are transactions that did not happen

Because that is a fair representation of the work. A brokerage that only publishes completed purchases is showing you a selected half of what it does, and the half it leaves out is the half where the advice mattered most.

We are paid on completed transactions, which means telling a client not to proceed costs us money in the short term. We do it anyway, and we would rather say so publicly than have a client discover the incentive for themselves halfway through a purchase.

None of these accounts should be read as a prediction. Every transaction turns on its own facts, and the fact that a bulkhead inspection saved one client money says nothing about what any other inspection will find.
