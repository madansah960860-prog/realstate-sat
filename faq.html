Frequently asked questions

The questions clients actually ask, answered directly. Where the honest answer is that we cannot help, that is what it says.

Working with us

Commission is negotiable. It is not set by law, by any association, or by custom, and there is no standard rate.

When we represent you as a buyer, we agree our fee with you in writing before we tour a property. Who actually pays it is separately negotiable: it may be offered by the seller, offered by the listing brokerage, paid by you directly, funded through a seller contribution to your closing costs, or some combination. If an offer of compensation is less than the fee you agreed with us, the difference is yours to pay. If it is more, we do not keep the excess.

When we represent a seller, the fee is agreed in the listing agreement and paid from your proceeds at closing.

Fair housing

No, and we will decline if asked. The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits statements indicating a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin or disability, and many states add further protected characteristics.

Describing who lives in a neighbourhood, or characterising a neighbourhood as suitable for a particular kind of household, would breach that. We describe the property, the amenities, the setting and the measurable facts. If you want demographic data, the US Census Bureau publishes it.

Fair Housing Notice

None of the four communities on this site is described as age-restricted, and none of them makes an age-based occupancy claim.

Age-restricted housing is a narrow exemption under the Housing for Older Persons Act with strict occupancy and verification requirements. A community only qualifies if it actually meets them and can document it. We will not apply that label to a community casually, and where a community claims it, we ask for the documentation and treat the claim as unverified until we have seen it.

Because a distance is a fact and a judgement about walkability is not. What is a short walk for one person is not for another, and phrasing that assumes a particular physical capability is both inaccurate and, in a housing context, a fair housing problem.

So we write "2.3 miles to the nearest full grocery, with a continuous sidewalk for the first 0.6 miles and none after that" and let you decide what that means for you.

What actually drives a coastal premium

From consultation to closing, most of our transactions run three to six months, with much of that spent before an offer rather than after one.

Under contract, a cash purchase of a single-family home can close in three to four weeks. A financed coastal condominium can take considerably longer, because the lender must approve the building as well as the borrower, insurance may take weeks to bind, and association paperwork moves at its own pace.
