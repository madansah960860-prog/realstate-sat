What the numbers describe

County-wide, this quarter's data shows modest movement rather than dramatic movement. Closed volume rose, median price rose slightly, price per square foot rose slightly, and inventory and days on market both increased. Months of supply moved from 3.7 to 4.1, which is a shift in balance rather than a break in it.

The submarket table shows the pattern that matters more for our clients: liquidity thins sharply above one million dollars, and thins again above five. At forty-one closed sales in a quarter, the McDowell foothills segment is small enough that we report the count prominently and would not draw a conclusion from any percentage change in it.

Lot sales are the segment worth watching for anyone considering building. Closed volume fell, median price was broadly flat, and median days on market is measured in months rather than weeks. Lots are a slower, thinner market than finished houses in every quarter we have measured, which affects both what you can negotiate on the way in and how long an exit takes.

Again: this describes what happened. It says nothing about what happens next, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Three structural factors specific to this market

Seasonality is pronounced and predictable. Listing activity and buyer activity both concentrate from October through April. Summer is genuinely quiet. A property listed in July competes with fewer listings and also attracts far fewer buyers, and the net effect varies by segment rather than being uniformly good or bad.

Build cost and lot economics. For hillside lots the disturbance envelope, the slope, the utility runs and the design review process all affect what a lot is worth independently of its acreage. We have seen two adjacent lots of similar size differ substantially in value because one had a usable pad and the other required extensive engineering. Anyone valuing a lot on price per acre is valuing the wrong thing.

Water supply designation. For newer development at the metropolitan fringe, whether a community sits within a designated assured water supply area has become a question buyers ask and lenders occasionally ask. It is not a factor in the established areas served by the large municipal providers, and it is a real question further out. We check it as part of buyer diligence. See the Arizona state guide.

What this report cannot tell you

  • What any specific property or lot is worth.
  • Where prices go next. We do not forecast.
  • Whether now is a good time for you. That depends on your circumstances.
  • What is happening inside a single gated community, where the sample in any quarter is usually in single figures.
